A tiny program that writes two lines explaining an everyday thing to a 10-year-old: one vivid sentence built on a single analogy, and one sentence saying what the analogy maps and what it leaves out. The entry is 207 characters: a traffic jam is a parking lot that forgot it was a road.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when set). Standard library only, no network, deterministic: two runs produce byte-identical output.

## The two lines

> A traffic jam is a parking lot that forgot it was a road.
> It maps rows of cars standing still, but leaves out that the cars all want to move and are stuck because too many of them are trying to use one road.

Thirty-six candidate pairs were written from deliberately different angles, the cliches were thrown out, and the survivors were judged pairwise against the brief by four readers — a literalist weighing whether the picture leaves the right idea, a novelty hunter weighing the unmet angle, a one-read reader weighing how fast the picture lands, and an editor weighing every word and the second line's honesty. The parking-lot line won the final 2-2 on the brief's criterion order: its joining is the one no reader had met before.
